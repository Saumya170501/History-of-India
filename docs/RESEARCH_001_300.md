# 1–300 CE: first research pass

Status: **36 records added; preliminary, not exhaustive or independently reviewed.** The collection now has 67 records and 17 sources. No historical geometry was inferred. Newly added records are timeline-only until individual place associations and coordinates are verified.

## Coverage added

- Kushan rulers: Kujula Kadphises, Wima Takto, Wima Kadphises, Kanishka I, Huvishka, Vasudeva I, Kanishka II, Vasishka and Kanishka III.
- Satrapal entries: Sodasa, Chastana, Nahapana, Rudradaman and Rudrasimha I. Some are dated attestations, not reconstructed full reigns.
- Later Satavahana sequence: Gautamiputra Sri Satakarni, Vasishthiputra Sri Pulumavi, Vasishthiputra Sri Satakarni, Yajna Satakarni, Vijaya, Canda and Mathariputra Pulumavi.
- Andhra Ikshvakus: Sri Camtamula, Virapurushadatta, Ehuvala Camtamula and Rudrapurushadatta.
- Context: Kushan, later Satavahana, Western Kshatrapa and Ikshvaku political traditions; early Chera, Chola and Pandya chiefdoms.
- Evidence and culture: a British Museum overstruck coin, Kushan religious coin imagery, Sangam literature and Nagarjuna.

Each application entry contains its own citation and locator. Museum and university sources are modern interpretations; a citation does not make every interpretation certain.

## Chronology decisions

The Harvard-hosted timeline supplies a convenient source-specific ruler sequence, with references to Cribb and Ollett. It is not treated as a universal chronology. For Gautamiputra, the selected table's c. 60–84 differs from INFLIBNET's 75–106 and the British Museum object's approximate 60–90. These alternatives remain visible in the record. Pulumavi and Yajna also have explicit alternative ranges. The ranges are not averaged or joined into an invented longer reign.

Consequently a filter result represents the chronology selected by that entry; it does not yet search all alternative chronologies. The next data-model improvement should support separate, sourced date assertions and let readers compare them without double-counting rulers.

Kanishka I uses Skinner's public abstract, corroborated by the timeline, for c. 127–150. Only the abstract was accessed; access to the paywalled full chapter is not claimed. Dates stated as approximate attestations remain labelled as such. A ruler appearing around one year is not claimed to have ruled for only that year.

Rudrapurushadatta's interval continues to 315 and has not been truncated to fit the batch. Broad first-century windows are contextual periods rather than assertions that a polity was founded in 1 CE. Early Tamil chiefdoms predate this atlas; their 1–300 windows represent the relevant CE segment, with that limitation stated in the records. They must not be equated to the later imperial Chola state.

## Evidence limitations and next work within this queue

1. Obtain full scholarly editions and page references for every chronological table; compare numismatic and inscriptional dates independently.
2. Complete the omitted Satavahana sequence, including Gautamiputra Siva Satakarni, Sivasri Pulumavi and Sriskanda Satakarni. Investigate Hala's literary association without manufacturing a reign interval.
3. Research Rajuvula, Bhumaka, Aubheraka, Jayadaman and Damajadasri. Several lack dates in the consulted table; keep these candidates out of year-filtered records until the dating model can honestly represent them.
4. Add Indo-Parthian and late Indo-Greek rulers, local northern polities, tribal/republican coin issuers and regional successors. This pass does not inventory all contemporary powers.
5. Verify named early Tamil rulers such as Karikala, Senguttuvan and Neduncheliyan against critical literary and epigraphic studies; do not import precise reigns from unreferenced lists.
6. The IGNOU Tamilakam entries currently rely on the indexed section 1.8 passage. Full PDF retrieval failed during research; obtain and inspect the complete edition before claiming page-level verification.
7. Read the Junagadh inscription in a scholarly edition before expanding Rudradaman's entry into military and irrigation claims. Similarly separate the Nasik eulogy's royal claims from independent evidence.
8. Add independently supported site coordinates, capitals and event locations. A modern museum holding a coin is not the historical location of its issuer.
9. Develop claim-level biography fields: succession, family, administration, grants, religion, commerce, campaigns, literature and source conflicts. Current short catalogue entries are not full biographies.
