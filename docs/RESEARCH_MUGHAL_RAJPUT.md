# Mughal and Rajput research expansion

Research pass: 7 November 2026. Batch `mughal-rajput-01` adds 50 records and enriches six existing Mughal reign records. The atlas now contains 117 records and 41 sources. This is an initial expansion, not exhaustive coverage or independent historical review.

## Coverage

- Mughal: Babur through Bahadur Shah Zafar, separate interrupted reigns, and selected succession claimants/interludes. Many later entries currently establish chronology only; they are not complete biographies.
- Rajput: selected Chauhan, Mewar, Marwar, Amber/Jaipur, Bikaner, Kota and Jaisalmer figures. Profiles include Prithviraj III, Kumbha, Sanga, Udai Singh II, Pratap, Amar Singh I, Jodha, Maldeo, Man Singh of Amber and Jai Singh II. Foundation events for Bika and Jaisal are not full reign windows.
- Conflicts: separate Tarain battles (1191 and 1192), Panipat (1526 and 1556), Khanwa, Chittor and Ranthambore sieges, Haldighati, Dewair and the 1857 siege of Delhi. No verified battlefield coordinates are claimed in these additions.
- Jaipur foundation/planning has its own sourced city point. It is not reused as a ruler's location or territorial boundary.

Use the collection browser to jump to a profile, then use the year controls to explore contemporary records. A profile may discuss events outside the selected year; the interface states that distinction. Battle context is year-filtered independently of catalogue search.

## Evidence and limitations

The source registry links the consulted pages with access dates. Each record and profile section carries locators and a statement of what the citation supports. Sources include Harvard's South Asian history chronology, Encyclopaedia Iranica, the Metropolitan Museum, V&A, UNESCO, Mehrangarh Museum Trust, Maharana of Mewar Charitable Foundation, Cleveland Museum, Asian Art Museum, Sarmaya and the National Army Museum.

Museum object dates must not become reign dates: Gaj Singh and Madho Singh portraits were produced later than the rulers' lives. Institutional lineage narratives are useful evidence but are not independent adjudication of dynastic claims. The Prithviraj entry uses a secondary report of scholarship and distinguishes later literary traditions from contemporary testimony. Rajasthan Tourism foundation statements for Bikaner and Jaisalmer were available through indexed excerpts; full-page retrieval was unsuccessful and this limitation is retained in their citations.

Conflicts and temporal qualifications are retained rather than silently reconciled:

- Rafi al-Darajat: Harvard's 1719 chronology versus the Met table's 1713–1719 entry.
- Shah Jahan II and Rafi al-Daulah refer to one ruler, supported by Sarmaya's coin catalogue.
- Vikramaditya and Udai Singh II: conflicting dates within the consulted Mewar material.
- Anup Singh: Cleveland's 1669–1698 versus the Asian Art Museum's 1674–1698.
- Man Singh of Amber: a service/career window, not accession dates.
- Ajit Singh: the recovered-rule phase, not every earlier claim.
- Bahadur Shah II: a table endpoint of 1858 does not imply effective rule after Delhi's capture in September 1857.

No battle casualty totals or definitive disputed outcomes have been supplied without evidence. A year window does not mean continuous fighting throughout that year.

## Maps

Two additional Harvard chronology map references supply broad Mughal and regional context. These are external links, not per-ruler reconstructions. The chronology warns that reproduction of the Schwartzberg atlas maps requires prior written permission. No map images were copied or digitized, and there are still zero historical territorial polygons.

Jaipur's city coordinate converts UNESCO's N26 55 27.4, E75 49 18.7 to latitude 26.924278, longitude 75.821861. This identifies a city, not the extent of Jai Singh II's kingdom. Other new ruler and conflict entries remain timeline-only pending geographic verification.

## Remaining scope

Systematic Rajput coverage still requires Bundi, Kishangarh, Sirohi, Dungarpur, Banswara, Pratapgarh, Karauli, Alwar, hill states and many earlier dynasties and branches, including work on Paramara, Pratihara, Chandella, Solanki, Vaghela, Gahadavala and Tomara material with careful historical classification. The current collections do not claim that every figure or dynasty used a single timeless identity.

Complete biographies require further research on succession and family, administration, economy, diplomacy, religion, patronage and campaigns. Mughal princes and rivals also need systematic coverage. Verified battle locations and licensed, dated maps require separate geographic research and independent review. Internet availability alone does not establish accuracy or permission to reproduce a map.

Validation and tests check citations, chronology filtering, aliases and geography rules; they cannot certify historical truth.
