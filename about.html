About and credits

A source-led atlas, not a finished border.

This atlas is a way to move through the history of the Indian subcontinent from 600 BCE to 2026 CE and read the evidence for each record. A year on the timeline is a search window. It is not a claim that a kingdom held the same land on every day inside that window.

Method

Records are added from published sources. A Wikipedia article, a museum page, or a surveyed coordinate can support a date or a point. Nothing is placed from memory alone. Where a kingdom has no surveyed frontier, the map may show an approximate outline traced from a free Commons map, or the nearest dated sheet. Those shapes are labelled approximate. A low fit is drawn softer than a closer one.

If a reign has no map of its own, it may share the dynasty’s outline. The note then says the extent is the dynasty’s, not that reign’s. A point is a cited place: where something happened, or a capital, not a territory.

Uncertainty stays visible. The year bar says when the political sheet is from another year. A sheet more than 30 years away is not drawn. Borders are not invented to fill the gap.
